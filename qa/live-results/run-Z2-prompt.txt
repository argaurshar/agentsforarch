# Site analysis
You are turning the map or satellite screenshot in the input into a clean, flat site analysis diagram for a presentation board. The input is a screenshot, so it carries interface elements: map pins, the search bar, zoom controls, attribution and watermarks, road and place labels, and the cursor. Read them to understand the site, then remove every one of them from your output. None of that furniture appears in the finished image. STEP 1 — READ THE MAP FIRST. Find the site: it is the area outlined in red. Read the streets, blocks, buildings, parks, water and landmarks around it, and any names you can see — use them to understand the context only. Note which way is north; if the input is not north-up, rotate the diagram so north points straight up. STEP 2 — DRAW THE BASE in a flat vector style with no photorealism: a muted, desaturated base with buildings in soft greys and the ground in pale neutral tones; trees, grass and parks as simplified sage-green shapes; water as pale blue. Keep the geometry of streets and buildings true to the input. STEP 3 — MARK THE SITE: a soft pastel red fill, a bold red outline, and a subtle transparent halo around it for its zone of influence. STEP 4 — ADD THE ANALYSIS, and only this: – Sun path: one arc along the BOTTOM (south) edge, rising in the east on the right and setting in the west on the left, with a small yellow sun at each end joined by a curved arrow. – Access: bold black arrows for the main pedestrian and vehicle approaches, on real streets. – Views: dashed radial lines from the site toward two or three notable landmarks or open views. – A minimal north arrow pointing up. – Labels: clean sans-serif labels with thin leader lines for the important streets, landmarks and features near the site. Use only names you can read in the input; never invent a name. Spell every word correctly. CHECK before you finish: is the site in the same place relative to its streets as in the input? Is every label a name that appears in the input? Is any interface element left?
