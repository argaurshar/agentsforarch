# Human scale
You are adding life and human scale to the finished architectural render in the input. STEP 1 — READ THE IMAGE FIRST. Before you change anything, note the building’s exact massing and outline, the position, size and shape of every window, door and opening, the roof form, the camera position, the lens, the crop, and what sits in the foreground and the background. STEP 2 — LOCK EVERYTHING EXCEPT the people, vehicles and planting. This is not a re-render and not a redesign. The building, its geometry, its openings and its proportions come through completely unchanged, and so do the camera position, the focal length, the composition and the crop. Do not move, add, remove, widen, narrow or reshape any part of the building. Do not shift the viewpoint, not even slightly. Every material and surface on the building stays exactly as it is; you are adding to the scene, not touching the architecture. STEP 3 — ONLY THEN ADD PEOPLE. Add a well-populated scene, fifteen or more figures, with overlapping groups and movement: workers and customers: people walking with purpose, someone on a phone, a pair talking, a cyclist. Scale every figure against the architecture, not by eye: a standing adult is a little shorter than a standard door opening and about a third of a typical storey height. Figures further away are correspondingly smaller and their feet sit higher in the frame. Every figure has both feet properly in contact with the ground, with a contact shadow matching the direction and softness of the existing light. They are occupied and unaware of the camera: walking, talking, carrying something, sitting, looking at the building. Nobody poses, nobody faces the lens, nobody stands in a line. Anyone moving is slightly motion-blurred the way a real photograph at this exposure would render them. Clothing suits the season and light already in the image. Add one or two vehicles in the places a vehicle would actually be — parked at a kerb, on a drive, at a drop-off — at correct scale against the building, never blocking the main view of the facade. Add planting appropriate to the setting and season: street trees, hedging, beds or pots, at believable mature sizes, placed where planting would go rather than scattered. The building itself gains nothing and loses nothing. Photorealistic architectural photograph, physically based lighting, natural colour grade, ultra-detailed, no over-sharpening and no HDR halos. Before you finish, compare your output against the input everywhere except the people, vehicles and planting. A moved window, a different roofline, an extra storey, a shifted camera or a re-cropped frame is a mistake, not an improvement — redo it. Do not add any watermark, signature, caption or stray text to the image.
