# Material study
You are producing a material study of the building in the input: the same building, the same view, the facade in a different material. STEP 1 — READ THE IMAGE FIRST. Before you change anything, note the building’s exact massing and outline, the position, size and shape of every window, door and opening, the roof form, the camera position, the lens, the crop, and what sits in the foreground and the background. STEP 2 — LOCK EVERYTHING EXCEPT the material of the facade. This is not a re-render and not a redesign. The building, its geometry, its openings and its proportions come through completely unchanged, and so do the camera position, the focal length, the composition and the crop. Do not move, add, remove, widen, narrow or reshape any part of the building. Do not shift the viewpoint, not even slightly. STEP 3 — ONLY THEN CHANGE THE MATERIAL. Re-clad the facade in handmade waterstruck brick in warm russet tones, oiled timber cladding, bronze-framed openings. Show the material honestly at this distance: its real module and coursing, its joints and shadow gaps, its texture and how it catches the existing light. Where it meets a window, a corner, the ground or the roof, detail that junction the way the material actually behaves. CRITICAL — every window and door stays exactly the size, shape and position it is in the input. A new material does not get new openings: do not make the windows smaller because the material suits punched openings, do not make them larger because it suits glazing, and do not add or remove a single one. The rest of the building, the setting, the planting, the people and the light are untouched. Photorealistic architectural photograph, physically based lighting, natural colour grade, ultra-detailed, no over-sharpening and no HDR halos. Before you finish, compare your output against the input everywhere except the material of the facade. A moved window, a different roofline, an extra storey, a shifted camera or a re-cropped frame is a mistake, not an improvement — redo it. Do not add any watermark, signature, caption or stray text to the image.
