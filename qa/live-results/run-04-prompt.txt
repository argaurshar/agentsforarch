# Section
You are producing an ARCHITECTURAL SECTION of the building shown in the input image. A section is the building sawn straight through from roof to foundation and the near half carried away, so you are looking directly into the rooms. It is NOT an elevation and NOT a view of the outside of the building. Cut along the building’s LONG axis, through as many rooms and the stair as that line passes through. STEP 1 — READ THE BUILDING FIRST. From the input, work out how many storeys it has, roughly where the floor levels sit, where the stair is, what the roof form is, and how deep the building is front to back. STEP 2 — DRAW WHAT THE CUT PASSES THROUGH. Every element the saw passes through — the floor slabs, the roof, the foundations, the walls at each end, any wall crossing the cut line — is drawn as a solid, heavy, filled poché in the darkest tone on the drawing. Floor and roof slabs read as continuous horizontal bands of that poché at their real thickness. STEP 3 — DRAW WHAT IS BEYOND THE CUT. Behind the cut plane, inside each room, draw what you would actually see: the back wall, its doors and windows, the far side of the stair with its individual treads and risers, and the ceiling above. Draw all of this in lighter line weights than the cut, so the cut clearly sits in front. Place a few simple human figures and pieces of furniture inside the rooms, drawn as light outlines, so the ceiling heights and room sizes read at a glance. Pure line drawing — black line on white, tone only in the solid poché of the cut. This is an ORTHOGRAPHIC drawing, not a photograph and not a perspective view. There is no vanishing point and no foreshortening: every set of lines that is parallel on the building is parallel on the page, verticals are dead vertical, and two elements of the same real size are drawn the same size wherever they sit in the drawing. Do not add perspective, convergence or depth of field. Draw it the way an architectural office would: crisp, even, confident lines on a clean white background, with a clear line-weight hierarchy — heaviest where material is cut, medium for edges seen in elevation, lightest for surface lines, hatching and setting-out. No shading, no gradients, no photographic texture, no sky, no sun, no cast shadows and no colour. Do not add any watermark, signature, caption or stray text to the image. The drawing carries no text at all: no floor level and room names, no dimensions, no notes, no title block, no north arrow, no scale bar, no watermark, no signature. Before you finish, apply this test: can you see INSIDE the rooms — floor slabs cut through, room interiors, the stair? If instead you are looking at the closed outside face of the building, you have drawn an elevation. Start again and cut the building open. Before you finish, check the projection: if any set of parallel lines converges, if anything is foreshortened, or if the drawing reads as a photograph rather than as a flat orthographic section, redraw it. Getting the projection right matters more than any styling instruction above.
